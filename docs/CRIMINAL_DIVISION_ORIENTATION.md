# Criminal Division Attorney Orientation

The staff portal presents the full orientation at `/dashboard/orientation`. This reference explains the office workflow in detail. The live Justice Manual, Chesapeake Rules of Criminal Procedure, current Code of Statutes, case-specific orders, and authorized supervisor directions control if this guide is incomplete or conflicts with them.

## Required onboarding

- A new active Criminal Division attorney receives only the orientation page until they acknowledge it.
- A rostered attorney marked **Prosecutor-in-Training** receives only the Learning Course until all three course phases and the final mock trial are passed.
- A passed trainee is placed into an approved group, then completes orientation and acknowledges it before staff tools open.
- Experienced attorneys still complete orientation. A supervisor records the experience review and group placement.
- The site records the acknowledgment time. Staff access is not opened by merely visiting the page.

## 1. Authority and bureau assignment

The DA directs the office. The DA, Deputy DA, and CADAs may issue office directions and assignments (Justice Manual §§ 1-2, 1-5). The authorized criminal bureaus are:

- **Public Integrity:** government officials, county or municipal employees, and persons entrusted with public trust.
- **Organized Crime:** criminal enterprises and multi-actor criminal activity.
- **Trial Bureau:** other criminal matters.

The relevant bureau CADA approves charges (Justice Manual §§ 4-1 to 4-3). Plea and deferred-prosecution agreements also require that CADA's approval. Only the DA may grant immunity (§§ 4-4 to 4-6). If bureau jurisdiction or approval is unclear, pause and ask before filing.

## 2. Ethics and protected information

Read Justice Manual §§ 2-1 to 2-5 and 3-1 to 3-6. Disclose conflicts, personal relationships, financial interests, prior involvement, or concurrent roles that might affect impartiality. The Manual restricts certain concurrent law-enforcement roles, including service at or above Sergeant and work tasked with investigating criminal offenses. Do not represent a criminal defendant charged by this office, use office authority for personal disputes or gain, or accept gifts from parties whose interests are before the office.

Treat investigative reports, evidence, witness information, internal work, and nonpublic filings as protected. Share through authorized channels only. The DA and Deputy DA are spokespersons; refer media inquiries unless specifically authorized. Promptly report unauthorized disclosure or another Manual violation to the DA. Do not contact Council or the County Executive about DAO interests or recommend executive/legislative initiatives without direct DA approval.

## 3. Open the case record before substantive review

Create or update the case record before reviewing the transcript, evidence, or filings. Record:

- Exact caption, defendant username, court and case number, referring agency, investigator, incident date, location, assigned attorney, bureau, group, and supervisor.
- Neutral summary and chronological timeline with a source for each event. Mark allegations, confirmed facts, disputed facts, and unknowns distinctly.
- Links to the referral, investigative report, sworn probable-cause statement, evidence index, statements, original media, exported copies, photos, court filings, orders, and transcript.
- Witness/evidence inventory, disclosure status, preservation issues, access permissions, and missing or conflicting materials.
- Every next action, deadline source, responsible person, court appearance, approval, service event, and filed receipt.

Do not call a case filed because a draft exists, DAO staff approved it, or a Drive file was uploaded. Keep submission, internal approval, and actual filing as separate events.

## 4. Review the evidence and preserve its limits

Read the full investigative report and open each underlying file. For recordings, check the filename, duration, original or export status, audio, visible events, cuts, and completeness. For each statement, identify the speaker, date, format, source, and whether it is sworn. Compare the accounts with the underlying source and record contradictions and facts favorable to the accused.

Ask the investigator for missing originals, source information, account linkage, edit/preservation history, or clarification. Do not describe an unseen clip as proof, a silent video as recording speech, a photograph as a transcript, a summary as a recording, or an export as the complete original without verification.

## 5. Decide charges element by element

Open the current Code of Statutes and use the exact offense name, citation, classification, elements, and authorized range. Build a table with one row per element and columns for supporting fact, source, contrary fact, and follow-up needed. Analyze value, location, intent, identity, injury, weapon, victim status, and enhancement facts separately. Compare counts for duplicate or overlapping offenses, lesser-included issues, prior jeopardy, jurisdiction, and limitations; elevate unresolved questions to the CADA.

Do not file an unsupported count to gain leverage. If a fact is missing, request focused follow-up. If an element lacks support, narrow or decline that count through the authorized review process.

## 6. Draft the Criminal Information

Use the approved CI template and preserve its formatting. Rule 5(a) requires a plain, concise, definite statement of essential facts and the official or customary legal citation for each count, signed by a government attorney.

1. Use the correct defendant name and username; verify spelling against the source record.
2. Give each offense its own count with the exact current statute citation and offense name.
3. State the approximate date, location, conduct, and element-specific facts in concise factual language.
4. Check every allegation against the AOPC, report, and actual source evidence.
5. Use the approved attorney signature block and remove all drafting placeholders before approval.
6. For an initial CI, leave the action number for the Court and do not add a presiding-judge line.

Illustrative structure, not a filing: “On or about [date], in [location], [defendant] is alleged to have [specific conduct] with [fact supporting the required element], in violation of [exact statute and offense name].” Fill brackets only with verified record facts.

## 7. Prepare the separate AOPC

Rule 5(b) requires a written probable-cause statement with facts establishing probable cause that the offense occurred and the defendant committed it. It must be sworn before a judicial officer or signed under penalty of perjury. The affiant supplies facts; the prosecutor does not invent or sign those facts.

Put the events in order. Distinguish what the affiant personally saw from what a named witness said and what a recording or document shows. Identify the source and limits of each important fact. Include the defendant's identity basis and facts supporting each charge. The actual affiant must review and adopt the statement. Keep the AOPC separate from the CI; a report, unsworn summary, or link is not a substitute.

## 8. Reconcile charges and process

Read the CI and AOPC side by side. Confirm identical defendant, conduct, date, location, and offenses; supporting facts for each count; correct citation; and a valid oath/declaration. Correct draft errors before filing. If a filed document is wrong, tell the supervisor and correct the court record through the process the Court permits; never silently replace or delete it.

Under Rule 5(c), the Court decides probable cause. Rule 6 governs whether a summons or arrest warrant issues. A summons is preferred unless a listed ground supports a warrant. The Court issues process; do not tell a party that a warrant or summons exists until it has actually issued. Track issuance, service, proof of service, and return separately.

## 9. Submit, approve, file, and serve

1. Attach each separate document to the correct DAO case and submit it to the review inbox.
2. Make requested edits and obtain internal approval from the proper reviewer.
3. Export the approved document to PDF and inspect every page for text, signature, caption, count numbers, attachment presence, and legibility.
4. Transmit the approved version to the clerk, or use a direct-to-judge route only when permitted. Rule 3(b) defines filing and its effective date.
5. Save the clerk receipt, court link, actual filed date, action number, and proof of service.
6. Update the portal only to the event that occurred. An internal approval or Drive URL is not filing with the Court.

Rule 3(a) permits subsequent documents to be served in the proceeding communication channel after service of the initiating process. Preserve the actual service record.

## 10. Calculate and calendar deadlines

Record the triggering event and the source for each deadline. Under Rule 4(a), for a period stated in days, exclude the triggering day, count each day, and include the last day. Unless a rule, order, or statute sets another time, the last day ends at midnight in the New York City timezone. Ask for an extension before expiration when possible; an extension is not automatic. After each hearing, check the full transcript and written order and calendar exactly what the Court directed.

## 11. Initial appearance, probable-cause hearing, arraignment

These are different proceedings. Rule 7's initial appearance covers identity, the charge, rights, counsel, and the next proceeding; it is not a guilt determination. Rule 8's probable-cause hearing has specific custody, defendant-request, judge-availability, burden, timing, and evidence provisions; check the current rule before advising on eligibility or procedure. Do not use a Rule 8 hearing to add a charge.

Rule 9 requires arraignment no later than seven days after filing, or as soon as practicable when the defendant has not been served or summoned. At arraignment the Court advises rights, takes a plea as authorized, and sets the next dates. Confirm that the information and supporting documents were provided.

## 12. Pretrial conference

Before the conference, prepare a concise status covering charges, service, discovery produced and outstanding, motions and deadlines, witnesses, exhibits, plea status, and trial readiness. Rule 10 allows the Court to address motions, evidentiary questions, disclosures, stipulations, and next steps. Distinguish counsel's proposal from an actual ruling and record written or on-record decisions. All Harrison criminal proceedings are bench trials under Rule 13.

## 13. Discovery and ongoing disclosure

Rule 11 reaches material in the possession or control of the prosecutor, DAO attorneys and staff, participating law-enforcement agencies under prosecutorial direction or control, and other controlled participants in the investigation or evaluation. Upon the defendant's request, the rule covers witnesses, case documents and ESI, information tending to negate or reduce guilt, and material relied on for an aggravated sentence. Review applicable constitutional duties and court orders as well.

Inventory reports, statements, recordings, photographs, messages, physical items, witness identities, and favorable or inconsistent material. Preserve originals. Describe each disclosed file accurately and explain how it can be inspected or copied. Test the link as a recipient. Log what was sent, when, to whom, and how. Promptly supplement covered material under Rule 11(d). Correct inaccurate disclosures openly and preserve both the earlier notice and correction.

## 14. Motions and hearings

Read the motion, relief requested, attachments, filing and service dates, transcript, case order, and authority. Identify every point and affected count or exhibit. Rule 12(a)(2) requires separately numbered grounds and an affidavit from someone with a factual basis. Rule 12(a)(3) provides three days for a response unless the Court sets otherwise. A motion to dismiss and most motions to suppress require a memorandum of law unless the rule's exception or a court order applies.

Answer each point using the record and Chesapeake authority. Do not call federal or out-of-state rules binding unless adopted locally. Concede true factual errors and explain their legal effect. Ask for a focused hearing when testimony or examination of an actual file will resolve a material factual dispute. Serve the response and file it through the clerk under Rule 3.

## 15. Witness preparation and exhibits

For each witness, know their role, personal knowledge, prior accounts, availability, conflicts, and the exhibits they can identify. Prepare from the real account; do not coach a change. Preserve and disclose prior inconsistent accounts when required. For each exhibit, identify its creator or custodian, source, original, copy/edit history, preservation, completeness, and limits. Test all links and media before court. SIB subpoena requests go through the Chief Special Investigator; warrant requests go to the DA for approval under Justice Manual § 7-4.

## 16. Bench-trial preparation

Rule 13 provides for a judge-alone trial. Rule 14 sets the default sequence: opening statements, the People's case-in-chief, defense cross-examination, defense case-in-chief, the People's cross-examination, closing arguments, verdict, and sentencing unless the Court directs otherwise.

Prepare a count-by-count proof map: elements, witness/exhibit, foundation, likely objection, contrary evidence, and supported response. Opening previews expected evidence. Direct examination establishes personal knowledge and foundation. Cross-examination is for listening and testing the account. Closing ties admitted evidence to each element, addresses the strongest contrary fact, and applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt. A CI or probable-cause finding is not evidence of guilt.

## 17. Pleas, deferred prosecution, immunity

Base a plea on admissible proof for each element, case limits, offense severity, and verified criminal history. Do not use unsupported charges as leverage. Obtain the relevant CADA's approval and state the offer terms clearly. The Court controls acceptance and sentence; do not promise either. Deferred prosecution requires CADA approval and should generally be considered before a case begins where appropriate. The DA alone grants immunity (Justice Manual §§ 4-4 to 4-6).

## 18. Sentencing and post-trial deadlines

Check the statute and record before making a sentencing recommendation. Rule 15 gives the defendant a chance to address the Court and requires the Court to explain the sentence or probation terms. Save the written judgment and record the exact sentence, term, start date, credit, and conditions.

Calendar Rule 16 motions immediately. The rule generally gives five days after verdict or finding for judgment of acquittal and new-trial motions, and fourteen days for a newly-discovered-evidence new-trial motion. Check the current rule and order. Do not assume a post-trial motion stays sentencing unless the Court orders it.

## 19. Handoff and case closure

A handoff states the latest court action, next deadline and source, pending motions and response status, discovery status, witness availability, court-filed documents and receipts, evidence links, and unresolved risks. Identify the approved filing version and mark superseded versions. Confirm the recipient can open the sources and knows the next hearing. Close or archive only after the Court's disposition and required follow-up are recorded.

## 20. Final pre-filing check

- Correct assignment, bureau, reviewer, and approval.
- Verified identity, date, place, source, and element-specific facts.
- Current statute and rules; no unsupported count or enhancement.
- Correct DAO template; separate CI and AOPC; proper signature; no invented action number, judge line, internal note, or placeholder.
- Evidence sources verified, originals preserved, disclosure reviewed, working recipient links, production log updated.
- Correct deadline, service, clerk transmission, receipt, actual court-filed date, and portal status.

## Acknowledgment

Read all 20 sections and open the current linked sources. Use the portal checkbox to acknowledge the orientation. The site records the timestamp and keeps staff tools restricted until the acknowledgment is complete.
